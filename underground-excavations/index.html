Excavation work in Burnaby represents a critical phase in virtually every construction project, from single-family home foundations to large-scale commercial and infrastructure developments. The category encompasses all activities related to soil and rock removal, trenching, shoring, and the geotechnical design required to ensure these operations are performed safely and efficiently. Given Burnaby's position within the seismically active Lower Mainland of British Columbia, excavations are not merely about moving earth; they are complex engineering tasks that must account for ground stability, groundwater control, and the protection of adjacent structures and utilities. A well-planned excavation is the foundation upon which successful projects are built, quite literally, making this category essential for developers, municipal planners, and engineering consultants operating in the region.

The local geology of Burnaby is a defining factor in how excavations are approached. The city sits on a varied geological landscape shaped by glacial and post-glacial processes, including the Vashon Drift and Capilano Sediments. Much of the urbanized area is underlain by glacial till, a dense mixture of clay, silt, sand, and boulders that provides excellent bearing capacity but can be very challenging to excavate without heavy machinery. However, significant portions of Burnaby, particularly along the Fraser River foreshore and in low-lying areas, feature softer, compressible soils and peat deposits. These conditions require specialized geotechnical design of deep excavations to manage risks like basal heave, excessive settlement, and instability. The presence of a high groundwater table in many areas further complicates work, making dewatering and impermeable shoring systems a necessity rather than an option.

Regulatory compliance is a non-negotiable aspect of any excavation project in Burnaby. The primary governing document is the British Columbia Building Code (BCBC) 2024, which adopts the National Building Code of Canada with provincial modifications. Section 4.2 of the BCBC specifically addresses excavation, shoring, and foundation requirements, mandating that excavations be designed and supervised by a registered professional engineer when they exceed 1.2 meters in depth or pose a risk to public safety. WorkSafeBC's Occupational Health and Safety Regulation, particularly Part 20, imposes strict requirements for sloped and benched excavations, temporary protective structures, and worker safety protocols. Municipal bylaws from the City of Burnaby add another layer, requiring excavation permits, right-of-way protection plans, and adherence to the city's Erosion and Sediment Control Bylaw to protect local waterways and drainage systems.

The types of projects that demand professional excavation services in Burnaby are diverse. High-density residential and mixed-use developments, such as those transforming the Metrotown and Brentwood town centres, routinely involve deep excavations for underground parking garages, often extending four or more levels below grade. These projects rely heavily on robust shoring systems like soldier pile and lagging walls or secant pile walls, which are designed through a geotechnical design of deep excavations process. Linear infrastructure projects, including the installation of large-diameter storm sewers and water mains, require precise trenching and bedding techniques. Hillside construction on Burnaby Mountain or the slopes of Capitol Hill presents unique challenges, where cut-and-fill operations must be carefully balanced to prevent landslides. Even smaller-scale projects, like a residential laneway house with a basement, fall under this category when they involve significant soil removal near property lines.

Q&A

What are the main geotechnical risks associated with deep excavations in Burnaby?

The primary risks include ground instability from loose or soft soils, basal heave in deep clay or silt layers, and excessive settlement that can damage adjacent buildings and utilities. A high groundwater table in many areas increases the risk of inflow and piping erosion. Seismic-induced lateral spreading during an earthquake is also a critical design consideration given Burnaby's location in a high-seismic zone.

When is a professional engineer required for an excavation in Burnaby?

Under the BC Building Code 2024, a registered professional engineer must design and review excavations that exceed 1.2 meters in depth, are adjacent to public property or structures, or involve complex shoring systems. WorkSafeBC also mandates engineered design for any temporary protective structure over 3.6 meters high. The City of Burnaby will typically require a sealed engineering letter or drawing as part of the permit application process.

What types of shoring systems are commonly used for excavations in Burnaby's soil conditions?

Common shoring systems include soldier pile and lagging walls for medium-depth excavations in glacial till, secant or tangent pile walls for deep excavations with high groundwater, and soil nail walls for stable cuts in competent soils. For soft, compressible soils near the Fraser River, sheet pile walls or jet grouting may be necessary to control water and provide positive ground support during excavation.

How does the City of Burnaby regulate excavation and sediment control?

The City of Burnaby enforces an Erosion and Sediment Control Bylaw that requires an approved plan for any project disturbing soil. This includes measures like silt fencing, stabilized construction entrances, and sediment ponds. Excavation permits are required for any digging on city property or right-of-ways, and projects must comply with the city's noise bylaw and street use regulations, which often restrict working hours and traffic impacts.
